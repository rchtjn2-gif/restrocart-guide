# QR table stands and tent cards: small signs that get scanned

Every table has a few square centimetres that guests look at for the whole meal. Most restaurants fill it with a laminated sheet and tape. A well-designed QR stand or tent card in the same space gets guests to open the menu, order another round, pay faster and leave the review that brings in next month's customers.

## Give each sign one or two jobs

| Job | What it says | Where it links |
|---|---|---|
| Menu | "Scan for menu" | Your digital menu |
| Order and pay | "Scan to order, Table 7" / "Scan to pay" | Your ordering or payment system |
| Reviews and social | "Loved it? Review us" | Google review page, Instagram, TripAdvisor |
| Promotions | Weekday offer, happy hour, new menu | Usually no QR: the card is the message |

The most common mistake is asking one small card to do all four. A guest will scan one code. Nobody scans four.

## Design rules for a QR code people actually scan

- **Size**: at least 3 x 3 cm. 4 to 5 cm is safer in warm, dim restaurant lighting.
- **Contrast**: a dark code on a light background with a clear white margin. Avoid busy patterns and glossy finishes that catch glare.
- **A label that says what happens**: "Scan for menu", "Scan to pay", "Review us on Google". A code without a label is a puzzle.
- **Dynamic links**: print a short link you control, so you can change the destination when you switch ordering platform or menu URL. Reprinting forty stands because a link changed is an avoidable cost.
- **Table numbers**: if guests order from the table, print the number on the stand and, ideally, encode it in the link. It removes the most common ordering error.
- **Brand, but small**: logo at the top or on the base. The code and the label are the heroes.

## Choosing a format

| Format | Material | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Block stand with slot | Wooden or MDF base, printed card or acrylic insert | Cafes, casual dining, QR menus |
| Engraved wooden block | Solid wood, laser-engraved logo | Brands with wooden menus and coasters |
| Clear acrylic L- or T-stand | Acrylic with printed insert | Modern interiors, counters |
| Hanging card frame | Wooden frame with rings | Specials, events, bar menus |
| Folded tent card | Printed card, A-frame | Offers, festivals, seasonal menus |
| Flip cue cards | Spiral-bound set on a stand | Stories, conversation starters, games |

Wood and acrylic bases look deliberate. Laminated paper looks temporary.

## Tent cards: rotate them

Tent cards sell specials and events. They work when they carry one offer, in large type, and when they change. Rotate them monthly so regulars notice them again.
