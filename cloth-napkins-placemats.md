# Cloth napkins, placemats and runners for restaurants: fabric, branding and how many to order

Table linen is the part of a restaurant's look that guests touch most and notice least, until it goes wrong. A greyed napkin, a frayed hem or a placemat with a peeling logo says more about upkeep than any review reply can fix. Done well, linen quietly repeats your brand at every seat and makes a mid-priced table feel looked after.

RestroCart's [tableware catalogue](https://restrocart.com/catalogue/tableware/) groups this under **table linen**: napkins, placemats and table mats, runners, cutlery wraps and tea towels, in cotton, linen-blend, denim and easy-wash poly, with embroidered or printed branding. This guide walks through the decisions in the order you should make them.

## Step 1: Decide what the table actually needs

Not every outlet needs every piece. Start from the service style.

| Outlet | Usually needs | Often skips |
|---|---|---|
| Fine dining | Large cloth napkins, runners or full cloths, sometimes cutlery wraps for the pass | Wipe-clean placemats |
| Casual dining and family restaurants | Cloth or good paper napkins, wipe-clean placemats | Runners |
| Cafes and bakeries | Cutlery wraps or small napkins, a few runners for communal tables | Full table cloths |
| Bars and brewpubs | Leatherette mats at the bar, coasters, tea towels behind the counter | Large dinner napkins |
| Banquets and catering | Matching napkins in bulk, runners per table | Placemats |

If you are opening a new outlet, decide this with your interior designer before the tables arrive. Placemat size depends on tabletop size and cover spacing, and a runner that is too long for a two-top looks like a mistake.

## Step 2: Choose the fabric for the wash, not the photo

Every piece of restaurant linen goes through far more washes than home linen. Pick the fabric for how it will be laundered.

- **Cotton.** Soft, absorbent and takes embroidery well. It wrinkles and can shrink if washed hot, so ask for pre-shrunk fabric and press or tumble finish if you want a crisp fold. The classic choice for cloth napkins.
- **Linen-blend.** A slightly textured, relaxed look that suits cafes and modern Indian restaurants. It wrinkles by design; the creased look is part of the appeal, so it is forgiving for teams without an iron.
- **Denim.** Heavy and durable, with a casual, crafted feel. Works for placemats, runners and cutlery wraps in cafes, burger bars and brewpubs. Dark washes can bleed in the first few washes, so wash them separately to begin with.
- **Easy-wash poly and poly-blends.** Hold colour, resist wrinkles and dry fast. The practical choice for high-turnover service and banquets, though less absorbent than cotton as a napkin.
- **Leatherette (for mats).** Not a fabric you launder; you wipe it. Leatherette placemats and table mats with a debossed or foiled corner logo suit bars and premium casual dining, and pair naturally with matching leatherette coasters and bill folders.

A useful rule: **if it goes in the washing machine, choose fabric; if it is wiped down between covers, choose leatherette or another wipe-clean material.** Mixing the two in one order is common, for example cloth napkins with leatherette mats.

## Step 3: Pick colours that hide work

White linen looks wonderful and shows every stain. If your menu is heavy on curry, tandoori marinades, turmeric or red wine, white napkins will turn cream in weeks unless your laundry uses strong stain treatment. Deeper shades such as maroon, bottle green, chocolate brown, olive, teal, rust or charcoal hide everyday marks and still look rich on the table. RestroCart's [table napkin work gallery](https://restrocart.com/work/table-napkins/) shows many of these: rust napkins with a gold embroidered flower, bottle green with a white script name, black with a small red monogram, teal with a gold round monogram.

If you want white for a fine-dining look, budget for a higher replacement rate and a professional laundry.

## Step 4: Brand it so the logo survives laundering

Branding is where cheap linen fails first. Three options cover most needs:

1. **Embroidery.** The most durable option for anything that is machine washed. A small corner logo or monogram in one or two thread colours looks premium and survives commercial laundering. RestroCart's own tableware FAQ recommends embroidery or high-temperature-safe prints for napkins so the branding lasts.
2. **Print.** Better for large artwork across runners or cutlery wraps, or for many colours. Make sure the print method is rated for the wash temperature your laundry uses.
3. **Debossing or foil.** For leatherette mats. Debossing presses the logo into the surface and cannot peel; foil adds colour or a gold finish.

Keep napkin branding small and in a corner. A napkin is folded, used and crumpled; a big centre logo disappears in use and costs more to stitch. One corner logo that shows when the napkin is folded on the plate is enough.

## Step 5: Work out quantities with par stock

The most common mistake with linen is ordering exactly one napkin per seat. On a busy weekend you will run out the moment a laundry batch is late.

Hospitality teams usually think in **par levels**: how many full sets you need so one is on the tables, one is at the laundry and one or more are clean in reserve. A practical starting point for cloth napkins is three to four per seat, adjusted for how many times you turn tables in a day and how fast your laundry returns. A worked example:

- 30 seats, tables turn about three times on a busy day, so roughly 90 napkins used per day.
- Laundry returns the next day, so you need a day on the tables, a day at the laundry and a buffer.
- That points to around 250 to 300 napkins rather than 30.

Placemats and leatherette mats are wiped, not laundered, so one per seat plus about 10 to 20 percent spares for damage is usually enough. Runners sit between the two: one per table plus a spare set.

Order spares from the same batch. Dye lots vary, and a napkin from a reorder six months later can be a slightly different shade from the originals.

## Step 6: Plan the laundry loop

Linen lasts longer when it is handled as a system:

- Separate linen from uniforms, and separate dark new linen from light pieces for the first few washes.
- Treat food stains before washing; turmeric and oil set with heat.
- Avoid harsh bleach on coloured napkins; it weakens fibres and fades embroidery thread.
- Store clean linen folded and covered, away from the kitchen.

## Step 7: Match the set

Linen looks best when it belongs to a family with the rest of the table: coasters, bill folders, menus and staff aprons in related colours and the same logo treatment. If you are ordering napkins, it is a good moment to check whether your coasters and bill folders still match. ## A quick brief to send your supplier

Before asking for a quote, write down:

- Pieces needed (napkins, placemats, runners, cutlery wraps, tea towels) and the size of your tables.
- Number of seats, table turns on a busy day and your laundry turnaround.
- Fabric preference, colour and whether you need wipe-clean mats.
- Logo file (vector if you have it), placement and thread or foil colours.
- Your opening or relaunch date.
