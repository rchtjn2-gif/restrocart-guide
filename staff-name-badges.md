# Staff name badges for restaurants: materials, fixings and what to print

A name badge is the cheapest item in a hospitality supply order and one of the few that changes how guests behave. A guest who can say "Ravi, could we get some water?" talks to your team differently from one who has to wave and say "excuse me". Named staff get thanked by name in reviews, and staff with their name on their chest tend to take a little more ownership of the table.

## Pick the material by the room

| Material | Look | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Acrylic, printed or laser-engraved | Clean, any brand colour, gloss, matte or metallic | The default for most restaurants and cafes |
| Metal, laser-cut (brass, steel, anodised aluminium) | Premium, heavier | Hotels, fine dining, managers |
| PVC | Light and cheap | Events, casual staff, high-turnover teams |
| Wood or bamboo, laser-engraved | Warm, natural | Cafes and natural-palette brands |

## The reusable slot badge

If your team changes often, use a badge body that carries the logo and a printed insert that slides in with the name. When someone leaves, you print a new insert, not a new badge. The new joiner gets a proper badge on day one instead of in week three, which is exactly when a guest is most likely to need to know who they are talking to.

A useful rule from RestroCart's workshop: **order about 25% more badges than you have staff**, so the gap between a hire starting and a badge arriving disappears.

## Fixings

- **Magnetic backs**: what staff prefer. No pinholes in knitwear or fine shirting, and easy to move between garments. Use for service staff.
- **Pins**: cheaper and more secure in physical roles, so use them in the kitchen and back of house, where a magnet can be knocked loose.
- **Crocodile clips**: for aprons and lanyards.

## What to put on it

1. **First name**, large enough to read from a metre away.
2. **Role**, smaller, where it helps the guest: manager, hostess, sommelier.
3. **A small logo**, only if the uniform does not already carry one.

Leave off surnames, employee numbers and taglines. They make the badge unreadable at the distance it is actually read from, and staff are less comfortable wearing a surname.

## Finishing the set

A metal lapel pin in the brand mark for managers, and tie pins for formal service, cost little and photograph well. They are usually made alongside the badges.

## Ordering checklist

- Staff list with first names and roles
- Vector logo
- Material and finish for each team (service, kitchen, management)
- Fixing per role (magnet or pin)
- 25% spare blanks or inserts
