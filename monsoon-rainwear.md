# Monsoon rainwear for restaurant staff

Delivery orders spike the moment it rains, valets run between the porch and the car park every few minutes, and the staff who receive supplies or work an outdoor wash area are soaked within the hour. A market raincoat lasts a week. The right rainwear, properly branded, lasts the season and turns riders into moving signage.

## Match the garment to the role

| Role | Best garment | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery riders | Two-piece rain suit: hooded jacket and trousers | On a two-wheeler rain comes from every angle; trousers keep the lap dry |
| Valets and doormen | Long hooded raincoat, knee length or below | Quick on and off over the uniform |
| Terrace and outdoor servers | Light hooded windcheater or packable rain jacket | Short dashes in drizzle; must not look heavy near guests |
| Security and gate staff | Long raincoat or rain suit | Long periods outside, often without cover |
| Dishwash, receiving, outdoor prep | Waterproof bib apron, sometimes sleeve covers | Splashes and spray rather than rain |
| Kitchen staff crossing open yards | Shared hooded ponchos kept at the door | Occasional use |

## Waterproof is not water-resistant

- **PVC-coated polyester**: fully waterproof, durable, wipes clean. Heavier and less breathable, so it can feel sticky in humidity. Good for valets and security who stand in the rain.
- **PU-coated polyester or nylon**: waterproof, lighter and softer, folds small into a pouch. The best all-round choice for riders.
- **Taffeta or microfibre with a water-repellent finish**: sheds drizzle and blocks wind, but steady rain soaks through at seams and shoulders. That is a windcheater, not a raincoat.

Ask which of these you are getting, and test the sample under a running tap before approving the order.

## Construction details to check

- A **storm flap** over the zip
- **Sealed or taped seams** at the shoulders and hood
- A **hood** that fits over a helmet for riders, or a peak for valets
- **Reflective tape** for riders on the road at night

## Branding: print, don't embroider

Embroidery punches needle holes through the waterproof layer. Print the logo instead. A large back print with your name, website or order number is the version that works as advertising.

## Timing

Brief your supplier in **April or May**, so rainwear arrives before the monsoon reaches your city. Ordering in the first week of rain means your team works the busiest delivery weeks unprotected.
