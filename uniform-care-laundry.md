# Make restaurant uniforms last twice as long

Most restaurants replace uniforms far more often than they need to. The garment rarely wears out from hard work. It gets ruined in the laundry, or it simply never comes back. Fixing that is cheaper than any fabric upgrade.

## What actually kills a uniform

- **Washing too hot.** 60 to 90 °C on every cycle fades dye, weakens fibres and cracks printed logos. Most everyday soil comes out at 40 °C.
- **Bleach on coloured fabric.** One enthusiastic bleach cycle can mottle a whole batch of coloured aprons for good.
- **Tumble-drying stained garments.** Heat sets oil and protein stains permanently. Always check before drying.
- **Over-drying.** Cotton shrinks and goes brittle. Line-dry in shade where you can.
- **Loss.** Not a laundry problem at all, but often the biggest single reason uniforms "wear out".

## The right wash temperature

For most poly-cotton uniforms and aprons, **40 °C** is enough. Keep **60 °C** for items where hygiene rules call for it, such as heavily soiled kitchen wear. Higher temperatures shorten fabric life and are the main cause of cracked prints and faded colours.

## Build a rotation, not a pile

Three tops per person is the standard for a reason: one on, one in the wash, one in reserve. With only two, every wash is urgent, and urgent washing is exactly when people wash too hot and dry too fast. A third set gives a day of slack and measurably longer life.

## A three-step stain routine staff will follow

Print this on a laminated card and put it by the lockers:

1. **Blot, do not rub.** Rubbing pushes oil deeper into the weave.
2. **Detergent straight onto the mark.** A small drop of dish detergent on oil or curry stains, worked in gently, left for ten minutes.
3. **Wash at 40 °C, air-dry, check before pressing.** If the mark survives, repeat before it ever sees a dryer.

Treat stains within the shift, not at the end of the week. Turmeric and tomato gravies are much easier to lift while fresh.

## Stop uniforms going missing

Named cotton laundry bags, one per person or one per cycle (dry clean, laundry, pressing), are a simple and effective anti-loss measure. Aprons vanish from a shared hamper and almost never from a named bag. Give each person a hook or shelf slot with their name, and the uniform starts being treated as theirs.

## Storage

Hang shirts, chef coats and waistcoats. Fold T-shirts and aprons. Never store anything damp: an apron folded wet after a Sunday shift will smell within days and can mildew within a week.

## If staff wash at home

Taking uniforms home is common in Indian restaurants, and it is also where wash conditions vary most. If you allow it, give each person a printed wash-care card and a named laundry bag. A woven wash-care label inside the collar or waistband removes most of the guesswork.

## Plan the top-up

Reorders often arrive half a shade off because the supplier no longer has the original fabric or embroidery file. Ask your supplier to keep patterns, fabric codes, dye lot references and embroidery files on record so a batch in month nine matches the first one.
