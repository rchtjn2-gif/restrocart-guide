# Choosing chef coat fabric for hot Indian kitchens

Indian kitchens are hard on uniforms. Tandoors, open flames and long summer shifts mean heat. Turmeric, oil and masala stain. And coats go through the wash every day. The right fabric makes a coat last months longer and keeps the line comfortable.

## What Indian kitchens demand

- **Breathability** for long shifts near heat.
- **Stain release** for turmeric, oil and tomato-based gravies.
- **Durability** under daily hot washes.
- **A clean look** for open kitchens where guests can see the team.

## Cotton vs poly-cotton vs blends

| Fabric | Good at | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| 100% cotton | Breathes well, soft, comfortable in heat | Wrinkles, can shrink, stains set if not treated quickly |
| Poly-cotton | Holds shape, dries fast, cheaper over many washes | Less breathable, can feel warm near the tandoor |
| Breathable blends / mesh panels | Airflow at the back or underarms | Mesh areas need gentler washing |

A common choice is a cotton-rich blend for the body, with ventilation panels for staff on the hottest stations.

## Fit, vents and closures

- **Double-breasted fronts** can be reversed to hide a stain mid-service.
- **Press studs or knot buttons** survive industrial washing better than ordinary buttons.
- **Back or underarm vents** help most on the grill and tandoor.
- **Sleeve length**: three-quarter sleeves keep cuffs out of food.

## Care routine that keeps whites white

1. Scrape and pre-treat stains straight after the shift.
2. Wash whites separately, and avoid overloading the machine.
3. Use oxygen-based bleach instead of strong chlorine, which yellows some blends.
4. Dry fully before storing to prevent mildew in humid months.
