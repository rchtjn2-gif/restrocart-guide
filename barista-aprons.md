# Barista aprons: what your coffee bar actually needs

Baristas wear an apron for the whole shift, so small design choices add up: neck strain, tools out of reach, logos that fade after a month.

## Cross-back vs neck strap

- **Neck strap** aprons are simple and adjustable, but put weight on the neck over long shifts.
- **Cross-back** aprons spread the weight across the shoulders and are usually more comfortable for full-day wear.

For staff on their feet for eight hours or more, cross-back is usually the better choice.

## Canvas, denim or leather

| Material | Pros | Cons |
|---|---|---|
| Canvas | Durable, easy to wash, takes printing and embroidery | Stiff at first |
| Denim | Hides coffee stains, looks good with age | Can bleed dye in early washes |
| Leather or leather trims | Premium look, long life | Cannot go in the washing machine; needs wiping |

## Pockets and tool loops

Think about what baristas actually carry: a thermometer, pens, a cloth, a phone, order slips. A chest pocket for pens, wide waist pockets and a towel loop cover most bars. Avoid too many pockets, which sag when full.

## Branding that survives washing

Embroidery generally lasts longer than printing on aprons washed often. Leather patches add a premium touch but must be removed or protected when washing. RestroCart's [barista apron guide](https://restrocart.com/blog/barista-aprons-guide/) goes into fabrics and branding options in more detail.

## Ordering for a small team

- Order a few extra for new joiners and laundry days.
- Choose adjustable straps so one size fits most staff.
- Ask for a sample or mockup before bulk production.
