# Sizing a restaurant uniform order: measurements, size sets and spares

Most uniform complaints are not about fabric or colour. They are about fit: sleeves that ride up when a server reaches across a table, shirts that are too short when tucked, trousers that pinch after a double shift. Almost all of these trace back to how the order was sized, and sizing is the cheapest part of the whole project to get right.

## Three mistakes that cause most fit problems

1. **Ordering by letter size.** S, M, L and XL mean different things to different makers, and Indian and international charts do not line up. A letter is a summary, not a measurement.
2. **Skipping the size set.** If you go straight to bulk, a sleeve that is two centimetres short is two centimetres short on every shirt in the order.
3. **Ordering exactly the headcount.** Someone joins in week three, or a shirt is ruined in week one, and there is nothing in stock that matches.

## The measurements to take

Do this once per person with a tape and record it in a shared sheet against each name. It takes about a minute each.

| Garment | Measure |
|---|---|
| Shirts, polos, chef coats, tees | Chest (fullest part), shoulder (seam to seam across the back), sleeve length, garment length from the high point of the shoulder |
| Formal shirts | Add collar size |
| Trousers | Waist, hip, inseam |
| Aprons | The wearer's height is usually enough to set the length |

Keep the sheet. You will use it for every reorder.

## Run a size set before bulk

A size set is one garment in each size, made before the main production run. Ask two or three staff with different builds to wear each piece and move the way they do on shift: reach up to a top shelf, carry a loaded tray, bend to serve at a low table, work the pass. Most size sets lead to one small adjustment. Each of those adjustments would otherwise have been repeated across the entire order.

## Use proper women's blocks

Shortening a men's shirt is not a women's fit. A women's block has a different armhole, shoulder slope, side shaping and hem. It costs about the same to produce and makes a noticeable difference to how the uniform is received by the team.

## Spares and dye lots

Order roughly 10 to 15 percent spares, weighted towards your two most common sizes, in the **same batch**. Fabric from a later dye lot can be a slightly different shade, which shows when a new hire stands next to the rest of the team.

## Make reorders easy

Ask your supplier to keep the pattern, fabric code, dye lot reference, thread shades and embroidery file on record. Then a top-up a year later is a short message with sizes and quantities, not a new project.

## A simple checklist

- [ ] Measurement sheet completed for every staff member
- [ ] Size set worn and tested in motion
- [ ] Separate men's and women's blocks confirmed
- [ ] 10 to 15 percent spares added in the same batch
- [ ] Supplier keeps patterns and files for reorders
