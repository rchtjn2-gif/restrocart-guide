# Kitchen uniforms by rank: a simple colour-coding system for a brigade

In a busy kitchen anyone should be able to tell who is in charge at a glance. A server with an allergen question needs the sous chef now, not after asking three people. Differentiating kitchen uniforms by rank solves this, but done badly it produces a kitchen that looks like five different restaurants.

## Why bother

- **Speed:** front of house finds the right decision-maker immediately.
- **Accountability:** names and designations show who signed off a dish or a temperature log.
- **Hygiene:** section colours on aprons make it obvious when someone has moved from raw meat to pastry without changing.
- **Motivation:** moving from commis to chef de partie is a real step, and a visible uniform change recognises it.
- **Guest perception:** in open kitchens, a clear hierarchy looks professional.

## The rule: one design language, a few variables

Keep the things that make the team look like one team, and change only one or two markers per rank.

| Keep the same for everyone | Vary by rank |
|---|---|
| Logo, embroidery style, thread colour | Coat colour (for one or two ranks only) |
| Fabric and fit | Collar or piping colour |
| Trouser colour and style | Closure: studs on the line, cloth buttons for seniors |
| Apron style within a section | Headwear: toque, skull cap or bandana |
| Size chart | Embroidered name and designation |

## A starting system

| Rank | Coat | Marker | Headwear |
|---|---|---|---|
| Executive chef | Double-breasted, full sleeve, best fabric | Distinct piping or collar, knot buttons, name and title embroidered | Tall toque, or none at the pass |
| Sous chef | Double-breasted or offset | Contrast collar or second piping colour, "Sous Chef" embroidered | Short toque or branded skull cap |
| Chef de partie | Offset single-breasted | Piping or cap colour by section, name embroidered | Cap in section colour |
| Demi chef / commis | Single-breasted, half sleeve | Plain team-colour coat, logo only | Skull cap or bandana |
| Trainee | As commis | Different cap colour or "Trainee" on the pocket | Skull cap |
| Stewarding | Polo or tee, not a chef coat | Different garment, dark colour, waterproof apron | Cap |

Adapt the titles to your own kitchen; the structure is what matters.

## Put section colours on cheap items

Section colour-coding (for example by station) works best on **aprons and caps**, which are cheaper to replace than coats. If you change your station layout later, you replace a few aprons, not the whole brigade's coats.

## Give stewarding real kit

The pot-wash and stewarding team often get old chef coats. That is uncomfortable and impractical for wet work. A dark polo or tee with a waterproof apron is cheaper, more comfortable and clearly signals a different role.

## Embroidered names are the cheapest marker

A name and designation above the chest pocket costs little per garment and removes all doubt. It also helps new staff learn names in their first week.
