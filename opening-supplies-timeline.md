# Opening a restaurant or cafe: a branded-supplies timeline that works backwards

Most opening checklists cover licences, kitchen equipment, hiring and the menu. The branded side, everything staff wear and every surface a guest touches, often gets squeezed into the final fortnight. That is exactly when supplier lead times catch up with you, and the team ends up training in plain T-shirts.

This guide sets out the order to do things in, working backwards from opening day. Exact lead times depend on the item and quantity, so always confirm them with your supplier's quote.

## Step zero: one brand file kit

Every category below depends on the same few files. Put them in one shared folder and send the same folder to every vendor. Most colour mismatches in new restaurants start with each vendor working from a different version of the logo.

- Logo in vector format (AI, EPS, SVG or vector PDF), plus a one-colour version and a small version for badges and caps.
- Brand colours as Pantone references and hex codes, with one or two secondary colours.
- Brand fonts (or at least their names) for menus, signage and print.
- Any pattern or illustration used in the interiors or on social media.
- Interior photos or renders: wall colours, furniture, lighting, table tops.
- The name of one person who approves artwork, samples and proofs.

## The timeline

| When | What to do | Why then |
|---|---|---|
| Week -12 | Finalise the brand file kit; list every role with headcount; count tables and covers | Everything else waits on these |
| Week -10 | Brief uniforms and ask for mockups for every role (include winterwear if you open before or during winter) | Longest lead time: design and sampling, then production |
| Week -9 | Brief signage and menu formats (board, folder, clipboard, paper); confirm table-top items | Signage needs site measurements; menus need samples |
| Week -8 | Approve uniform mockups; check physical fabric swatches | A delay here pushes everything back |
| Week -7 | Approve uniform samples and collect staff sizes; brief packaging if you deliver | Bulk production starts only after sample approval |
| Week -6 | Approve signage artwork and tableware samples (coasters, bill folders, tent cards) | Leaves time for production and installation |
| Week -4 | Order packaging and stationery; order name badges once names are confirmed | Final details are clearer by now |
| Week -3 to -1 | Print menus last; receive uniforms for training | Prices change late; staff need uniforms for mock service |

## What can wait, and what cannot

**Cannot wait:** uniforms (they must be in the building for training), menus and signage.

**Can wait until after opening:** merchandise such as tote bags and mugs, and most secondary print. Selling merchandise works better once you have regulars anyway.

## Three habits that save money

1. **Keep menus flexible.** Clip-style, screw-post or insert-based menus let you change prices without reprinting the whole cover. Late price changes are normal before opening.
2. **Order uniforms and table items together.** One supplier working from one brand kit keeps colours consistent across aprons, coasters and bill folders.
3. **Order spares.** New joiners, laundry days and lost coasters are certain. A small buffer now is cheaper than a rush reorder in month two.

## Quantities: a quick rule of thumb

For uniforms, plan three tops per person: one worn, one in the wash, one in reserve. For coasters, plan four to six per table for reusable materials plus a spares box. Name badges should be reusable (slot-in or engraved) so they outlive staff changes.
